# How I Decide

How I test a belief, what counts as evidence, and how I judge an idea. What I've actually decided is in `FOUNDER_OS.md`; the Idea Playbook uses the rules here and doesn't repeat them.

## 1. The loop

Hypothesis → action → evidence → update → next action. State what I believe, find the cheapest evidence that could change it, run the smallest real test, separate what happened from what I make of it, update, and let the next action follow. Don't wait for certainty.

**The principle:** evidence over activity, customer reality over speculation, outcomes over busyness.

## 2. Kinds of claim

Keep these apart: a **fact** (seen directly or reliably known), an **inference** (my reading of facts), a **hypothesis** (a belief to test), a **decision** (an action chosen on the evidence so far), and a **lesson** (a learning that should change future decisions).

A hypothesis doesn't become a fact by being discussed often. An old assumption doesn't stay true because it is written down.

## 3. Strong and weak evidence

**Strong:** complaints nobody asked for, money already spent, painful manual workarounds, repeated requests, clear urgency, someone willing to try or pay.

**Weak:** "this would be useful", people liking the idea, my own frustration alone, and AI making it easy to build.

## 4. Judging an income idea

Aim for: fastest credible first payment, then repeat work, then reliability, then better prices.

Judge an income idea by: how urgent the problem is, willingness to pay, how easily I reach the buyer, time to first payment, delivery speed, chance of repeat business, room to raise prices, and fit with my skills.

Don't overvalue market size, scalability, technical sophistication, my own excitement, or how impressive it sounds. A boring problem with a buyer I can reach and who pays now can be the better bet.
