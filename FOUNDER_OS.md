# Founder OS

> **Canonical source of truth for the Founder OS.**
>
> This file is the primary state of the Founder OS. The local AI agent
> should read it before making strategic recommendations, planning work,
> or changing the system.
>
> The OS is intentionally lean. It defines how I operate and what
> matters now; it is not a product specification, task manager, journal,
> or archive of every conversation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose

Founder OS is my personal operating system for making better decisions
and executing toward meaningful outcomes.

It should help me:

-   choose what to work on;
-   decide what **not** to work on;
-   allocate limited time and energy;
-   turn hypotheses into experiments;
-   learn from evidence;
-   maintain strategic continuity across conversations;
-   protect recovery and sustainable capacity.

The core loop is:

**Hypothesis → Action → Evidence → Update → Next Action**

The OS should become more useful as evidence accumulates, but should not
become bloated.

## 2. Current Strategic Priorities

### Priority 1 --- Income Track

**Immediate outcome: generate ₹60,000/month reliably.**

The near-term objective is not to build a theoretically scalable
business. It is to find and execute the fastest credible path to
reliable income.

Current principles:

-   Prefer customers with an existing need over products requiring
    demand creation.
-   Prioritize urgency, willingness to pay, accessibility of buyers,
    speed of delivery, and probability of first payment.
-   Use selling/outreach and real customer conversations to discover
    what people will pay for.
-   Do not spend substantial time building before validating demand.
-   Higher-ticket customers are preferable when the sales cycle remains
    credible.
-   Existing skills can be packaged into services when that creates the
    fastest route to revenue.

### Priority 2 --- Startup Discovery Track

**Find painful, recurring, costly problems worth solving with a scalable
product.**

This is deliberately separate from the Income Track.

The Startup Discovery Track should focus on:

-   real problems;
-   repeated behavior;
-   meaningful pain;
-   economic value;
-   identifiable users/buyers;
-   evidence of willingness to pay;
-   opportunities where software/product leverage could eventually
    matter.

Do not build a SaaS, AI bot, or other product merely because the idea
sounds interesting.

**Problem discovery precedes product development.**

### Priority 3 --- Content

Content supports the two primary tracks.

It can:

-   create distribution;
-   demonstrate expertise;
-   generate leads;
-   expose problems and customer language;
-   strengthen credibility.

Content is **not a separate primary outcome** unless that changes
through an explicit decision.

### Optional --- Side Projects / Jobs

Side projects and employment opportunities are allowed when
strategically useful.

They should not silently displace the primary priorities.

### Protected --- Recovery

Recovery is part of the operating system, not leftover time.

## 3. Current Strategic Decisions

These are decisions already made and should be treated as the current
default unless new evidence justifies revisiting them.

### Wave Link --- Paused

Wave Link is paused.

Reason:

-   The product was built without meaningful usage.
-   The important unresolved problem was not product implementation but
    finding a sufficiently painful customer problem / ICP.
-   Continuing to build would create activity without evidence of
    demand.

Lesson:

**Do not confuse having a working product with having a validated
problem.**

The core learning and operating loop should be preserved, but Wave Link
should not consume near-term Founder OS attention.

### Generic Website Cold Outreach --- Stopped

Cold outreach selling generic websites to dental clinics was stopped.

Reason:

-   A website is often a pull product for this customer segment rather
    than an urgent purchase.
-   Clinics may view websites primarily as trust/credibility assets
    rather than direct booking infrastructure.
-   The sales motion did not provide sufficiently strong evidence of
    urgent demand.

Important nuance:

**This does not mean websites are universally dead.**

The conclusion is narrower:

> Generic website outreach is currently a weak sales channel/product
> combination for this segment.

Website work can remain passive/referral-based rather than being a
primary outbound strategy.

### Premium Artist Landing Pages --- Exploration Only

Premium landing pages for elite artists were explored as a potentially
higher-ticket service, with an indicative starting range around ₹1--1.5
lakh.

This is **not a validated business direction**.

It remains an experiment/opportunity, not a strategic commitment.

## 5. Work Modes

Work should be classified by the cognitive mode it requires.

### Deep

For work requiring significant thinking, judgment, synthesis, or problem
solving.

Examples:

-   Think
-   Solve
-   Decide
-   Discuss
-   Strategy
-   Problem discovery
-   Important product decisions
-   Complex analysis

**Default rule:** protect high-energy periods for Deep work.

### Production

For turning decisions into concrete output.

Examples:

-   Build
-   Edit
-   Refine
-   Implement
-   Design
-   Write
-   Ship

Production is execution after sufficient clarity exists.

### Light

For lower-cognitive-load work.

Examples:

-   Research
-   Admin
-   Posting
-   Organizing
-   Routine communication
-   Simple data collection

Light work should not consume the best cognitive hours merely because it
is easy to start.

### Deep vs Production vs Light

The distinction is based on **cognitive demand**, not whether the work
happens on a computer.

For example:

-   Deciding what customer problem to pursue → **Deep**
-   Building the experiment for that decision → **Production**
-   Collecting a list of potential customers → **Light**
-   Calling those customers and adapting the pitch → potentially
    **Deep**, because the interaction requires judgment and learning

## 7. Income Track Operating Rules

The income track should optimize for:

**Fastest credible first payment → repeatability → reliability → better
economics.**

Evaluation criteria:

1.  Urgency of the problem
2.  Willingness to pay
3.  Ease of reaching the buyer
4.  Time to first payment
5.  Delivery speed
6.  Probability of repeat business
7.  Potential to increase pricing
8.  Fit with existing skills

Do not overvalue:

-   market size;
-   theoretical scalability;
-   technical sophistication;
-   personal excitement;
-   how impressive the solution sounds.

A boring problem with an accessible buyer and immediate willingness to
pay can be strategically superior.

Current known constraint:

-   Meaningful customer access has previously been limited.
-   Direct selling/outreach has been treated as a scarce resource and
    should be used deliberately rather than diffusely.

## 8. Startup Discovery Operating Rules

Startup discovery should follow evidence rather than ideation.

For each candidate problem, investigate:

-   Who experiences it?
-   How frequently?
-   How painful is it?
-   What do they currently do?
-   What does the current workaround cost?
-   What happens if they do nothing?
-   Who controls the budget?
-   Is there evidence they already spend money or significant effort?
-   Can the problem recur?
-   Can a product create meaningful leverage?

Strong evidence includes:

-   repeated unsolicited complaints;
-   existing spending;
-   painful manual workarounds;
-   repeated requests;
-   measurable economic loss;
-   clear urgency;
-   willingness to try/pay for a solution.

Weak evidence includes:

-   "This would be useful."
-   Personal frustration alone.
-   People saying they like an idea.
-   Building something and observing that nobody uses it.
-   AI making the idea technically easy.

## 9. Decision-Making Framework

When facing an important decision:

### 1. State the hypothesis

What do I currently believe?

### 2. Identify the cheapest useful evidence

What is the smallest action that could materially increase or decrease
confidence?

### 3. Act

Run the smallest credible experiment.

### 4. Observe

Separate actual evidence from interpretation.

### 5. Update

Change the hypothesis if evidence warrants it.

### 6. Choose the next action

The next action should follow from the updated evidence.

Avoid spending excessive time trying to achieve certainty before acting.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Evidence Discipline

Distinguish clearly between:

-   **Fact** --- directly observed or reliably established.
-   **Inference** --- interpretation of facts.
-   **Hypothesis** --- belief that needs testing.
-   **Decision** --- chosen action based on current evidence.
-   **Lesson** --- durable learning that should affect future decisions.

Do not turn hypotheses into facts simply because they have been
discussed repeatedly.

Do not preserve outdated assumptions merely because they are written in
the OS.

## 11. Weekly Operating Rhythm

The weekly system should answer:

### Outcomes

What must be true by the end of the week?

Outcomes should be concrete and connected to strategic priorities.

Example:

-   Make measurable progress toward ₹60k/month.
-   Run a defined number of customer/problem experiments.
-   Produce a specific piece of useful content if it supports the above.

### Bottleneck

What is currently preventing progress?

Choose the highest-leverage bottleneck rather than listing every
problem.

### Experiments

What hypotheses will be tested?

Each experiment should have:

-   hypothesis;
-   action;
-   evidence sought;
-   expected decision after evidence.

### Review

At the end of the week:

-   What happened?
-   What did I learn?
-   What changed?
-   What should stop?
-   What should continue?
-   What should be tested next?

The weekly plan should remain outcome-oriented rather than becoming a
long task list.

## 12. How the AI Agent Should Work With This OS

The local AI agent is a **thinking and execution partner**, not the
owner of Founder OS.

### Before giving strategic advice

Read this file and identify:

-   current priorities;
-   active decisions;
-   constraints;
-   relevant lessons;
-   unresolved hypotheses.

Do not resurrect an old idea simply because it appears in historical
context.

### When discussing a new idea

Classify it as one of:

-   Income opportunity
-   Startup discovery
-   Content support
-   Optional project
-   Personal/recovery
-   Unrelated distraction

Then assess it against the current hierarchy.

### When proposing a strategic change

Do not silently modify the OS.

Instead:

1.  Explain the proposed change.
2.  Explain the evidence/reasoning.
3.  Identify what existing decision or principle it changes.
4.  Ask for confirmation when the change is material.
5.  Only after agreement, update the canonical Markdown.

### When updating the OS

Prefer small, explicit changes.

Do not add:

-   unnecessary task lists;
-   verbose conversation transcripts;
-   speculative ideas;
-   implementation details that belong elsewhere;
-   duplicated information;
-   temporary thoughts that have not become durable context.

### When something is uncertain

Mark it as uncertain.

Do not manufacture certainty to make the OS look cleaner.

## 19. Historical Lessons Worth Preserving

### Lesson: Building is not validation

A working product can still have zero meaningful demand.

Before investing heavily in implementation, establish evidence that the
problem matters.

### Lesson: Sales-channel failure is not automatically market failure

Failure to sell generic websites through cold outreach to dental clinics
does not prove that websites have no value.

It indicates that the combination of:

**segment + offer + urgency + outbound channel**

was weak enough to stop pursuing.

### Lesson: Accessibility matters

An opportunity is more attractive when the buyer can be identified and
reached directly.

### Lesson: Price should follow value and buyer economics

Low-price customers can create activity without creating a viable income
path.

Where possible, test offers with customers who have stronger economics
and a meaningful reason to pay.

### Lesson: Protect discovery from building

Technical ability makes it easy to build prematurely.

The OS should actively prevent technical execution from becoming a
substitute for problem discovery.
